# OpsDesk — Demo Video Script (4:30, English)

Narration runs at about 150 words per minute, about 640 words in total. Each scene has an ID, so the voiceover, the screen recording and the editing can be synced scene by scene.

**Before recording:** Ops Lead → Team & routing → **Reset demo data**. Browser at 1920×1080, zoom 110%.

| # | Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 Hook** | 0:00–0:20 | App, agent view: booking TBOH-118204 with its contacts listed in the booking case panel | A travel agency asks TBO about the same refund. Then again. Then a third time. In today's ticketing system, that's three separate tickets, three different owners, and the agency explains the problem from scratch every time. |
| **S2 Problem** | 0:20–0:45 | **Deck: problem slide** | TBO's operations team lives with this every day. Tickets lose their context when they move between desks. Routing is first come, first served. And every ticket runs on the same clock, whether the guest checks in next month or tomorrow morning. TBO's real question isn't whether a system can manage tickets. It's whether it can handle B2B travel complexity, efficiently and economically. |
| **S3 Idea** | 0:45–1:15 | **Deck: solution / USP slide** | We built OpsDesk on two ideas. First, the booking is the memory. Every ticket, supplier conversation and past contact attaches to one case, keyed on the TBO booking ID and the agency. Second, the deadline follows the trip, not the ticket. Our SLA is the soonest of the priority timer, the check-in date and the last cancellation date, so as the trip gets closer, the deadline tightens by itself. |
| **S4 How** | 1:15–1:30 | **Deck: architecture slide** | Under the hood, one classifier labels each ticket once, and that single label drives routing, deadlines and insights. Rules decide anything involving money or time. AI only classifies. And humans stay on the risky edges. |
| **D1 Bookings** | 1:30–1:45 | Log in as Ravi (Skyline) → **My bookings** → hover over a policy table and its "now" row | Here's the agency's side. Ravi from Skyline Travels sees his bookings straight from TBO, each with its supplier cancellation policy. Nobody types in dates or deadlines. The last free cancellation date is calculated from the policy itself. |
| **D2 Chatbot** | 1:45–2:15 | **Chat with TBO support** → type *"Need to cancel a booking, the guest cannot travel"* → pick **TBOH-118999 · Coral Bay Resort** → summary → **Submit ticket** | Ravi opens the support assistant and simply describes the problem. OpsDesk recognises a cancellation and lists only Skyline's own bookings. He picks the Goa resort, checking in tomorrow. Before he submits, it shows the refund he'd get right now, and a response time of one hour, because the trip is so close. One click, and the ticket is raised and routed. |
| **D3 No duplicates** | 2:15–2:35 | Type *"Refund for TBOH-118204 is still not credited, third time asking"* → bot shows the *"Your colleague Meera already raised #2"* prompt → **Yes, add to #2** | Now the refund being chased for the third time. The assistant sees that Ravi's colleague Meera already raised this exact issue on the same booking, so it adds his message to her ticket instead of creating a duplicate. |
| **D4 Agent context** | 2:35–3:00 | Switch to **Rahul Verma** → open #2 → scroll: booking case, policy card → **Summarize this case** | On the agent side, Rahul opens the ticket and sees the whole booking case: every contact, who raised it, and the cancellation policy. One click summarises the history, so no one asks the agency to explain again. When tickets look like duplicates, OpsDesk suggests a merge, and an agent confirms it. |
| **D5 Routing** | 3:00–3:15 | Switch to **Marco Rossi** → New ticket → preset **Urgent EU check-in** → Create → highlight the routing reason | An urgent European ticket is routed by region, priority and availability. The senior agent gets it, the off-shift agent is skipped, and the reason is written on the ticket. |
| **D6 Side conversation** | 3:15–3:30 | Switch to **Arjun (Lead)** → open #5 → side conversation with 2 hotel emails → Owner → **Priya Sharma** → timeline line | Supplier conversations use explicit recipients, not the ticket's group. When this ticket moves to a new agent, the hotel thread stays intact. The recipients don't change. |
| **D7 Escalation** | 3:30–3:45 | Open #3 (At risk, check-in tomorrow) → type a reason → **Escalate to Escalation Manager** → switch to **Neha** → Escalation queue | This name change is at risk because the guest checks in tomorrow. One click escalates it straight to the escalation manager, who works it from her own queue. |
| **D8 Insights** | 3:45–4:00 | Switch to **Arjun** → **Insights & data** → themes, Atlas Bedbank incident → **Export all tickets** | Finally, the ops lead sees this week's top themes with suggested product fixes, spots a supplier outage hitting three agencies, and exports all fifty thousand tickets in one click. No three-thousand-row cap. |
| **S5 Close** | 4:00–4:30 | **Deck: closing / why-we-win slide**, then title card | OpsDesk turns isolated tickets into travel-aware cases. Rules decide money and time, AI only classifies, and humans confirm merges and own escalations. So it runs at a low, predictable cost, as one platform instead of a stack of paid add-ons. Linked by booking. Routed by reality. Escalated on purpose. |
